01 / INDUSTRIAL DESIGN

THE WATCHER

PROJECT DESCRIPTION

A desk companion designed to encourage healthier work habits through movement, expression and timely breaks.

Project information
TypeIndustrial Design
Year2026
RoleConcept, CAD, Design & Prototyping
ToolsFusion 360 / Blender / Adobe Illustrator / Photoshop
01 / Overview

A DESK COMPANION FOR HEALTHIER WORK HABITS

Long hours at a desk can lead to eye strain, poor posture and sitting still for too long. It is also easy to lose track of time, put off tasks or forget to drink water. THE WATCHER makes these habits easier to notice through expressive eyes, head movement and spoken prompts.

The camera tracks the user’s face and guides the servos inside the body, allowing the head to turn from side to side and tilt up and down. A separate distance sensor checks how close the user is to the screen so THE WATCHER can prompt them to adjust their position. The display in the base shows the time, timer settings and battery level in the proposed experience.

02 / Interaction

SET A WORK RHYTHM

The user sets a work period and break interval with the plus and minus buttons. For example, they could plan six hours of work with a reminder every 30 minutes. THE WATCHER is designed to announce breaks, suggest stretching or drinking water, and use its eyes to communicate different expressions.

The intended break routine also includes an optional function to close the laptop screen. The user can turn this off and keep the spoken reminders when they need to continue working uninterrupted.

03 / Ideation

FROM CHARACTER TO PRODUCT

Early sketches explored the cat silhouette, the relationship between head and base, and ways to fit the movement mechanism inside a compact body.

Character, form and movement studies.

04 / Development

DESIGNING THE MOVEMENT

I modeled the head and base as separate assemblies, with a mechanism designed to turn the head from side to side and tilt it up and down. This movement is intended to keep the camera and distance sensor directed toward the person at the desk.

The base holds the controls, display, speaker, USB C charging port and rechargeable batteries. The exploded view shows how the shells, supports and moving parts fit together.

Exploded view showing three colour variants and the designed assembly.

CAD OF THE MOVEMENT SYSTEM

Section and assembly views show the spur gears, the head support and the space reserved for the servos.

05 / Prototype

FROM CAD TO PHYSICAL PROTOTYPE

I designed and 3D printed the head, moving assembly and base, then assembled the mechanical and electrical parts myself. The physical prototype shows the product’s form, construction and illuminated eye graphics. Its rechargeable battery setup provides about six hours of prototype use.

Integrating the electronics exposed issues with the displays and speaker, and the head movement could not be demonstrated reliably. This build establishes the physical design and assembly, while the complete tracking and reminder sequence still needs further electrical work and testing.

The assembled prototype and 3D printed parts. The electrical behavior needs further development.

06 / Outcome

FINAL DESIGN

The final design brings the expressive head and functional base into one compact form. The eyes give the product its character, while the display, controls and speakers are integrated into the base. The views below show the front, side and back of the intended finished design.
